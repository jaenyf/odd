# Open Direct Democracy — Constitutional Framework (Draft)

## Preamble

We, the citizens participating in Open Direct Democracy, establish this framework to ensure that political power remains with the people.

No individual, political party, institution, organization or privileged group shall possess permanent authority over the collective.

Every citizen has the right and is encouraged to participate in shaping the laws, policies and direction of society.

The system shall remain open, transparent, accountable, secure and capable of continuous democratic improvement only by its interaction with individuals.

## Article 1 — Sovereignty of the people

- 1. Because of human nature, ODD will face attempts at sabotage, power grabs, deception, manipulation, and subversion—whether through deception, trickery, or direct attack—and must therefore ensure its own security in order to guarantee the security of all citizens. However, no security mechanism can justify the implementation of opaque processes that escape the scrutiny of even a single citizen, even if they pertain to national security. Fundamentals proposed by ODD’s lie in its openness and are the guarantee of the security, the freedom, and the application of a direct democracy by and for citizens.
- 2. There shall be no state; all political authority directly originates from individual citizens.
- 3. There shall be no moral or physical representatives, elected or not, that may exercise independent legislative authority on behalf of citizens.
- 4. Citizens have the right —and should be encouraged— to propose, debate, amend, and vote directly on laws and public policies.
- 5. Because of everyone's ego, citizens —and consequently the ODD— must guard against any desire to seize any form of power, whether it is —allegedly— justified or not.
- 6. All public institutions are tasked with implementing democratically approved decisions, not with overriding the will of the citizens. Their operations must be open, transparent, and traceable at any stage by any citizen.

## Article 2 — Equal citizenship

- 1. All citizens enjoy the same fundamental political rights regardless of their opinions, ideals, political or religious beliefs.
- 2. Every citizen has an equal basic voting right.
- 3. Reputation, expertise, wealth, popularity or social status cannot decrease or increase a citizen's fundamental rights.
- 4. ODD may reward constructive contributions, but reputation must never become a substitute for equal citizenship.
- 5. No citizen may be permanently excluded from participation, although societal safeguards may restrict it.

## Article 3 — Legislative direct democracy

- 1. All citizens have the right to:
    - Submit legislative proposals.
    - Discuss proposals publicly.
    - Suggest amendments.
    - Challenge existing laws.
    - Vote directly on legislative decisions.
    - Initiate reviews or repeal procedures.
- 2. All proposals and amendments must be made available to the citizens; no exceptions should be made on the grounds of confidentiality or security in this regard, as this could constitute a security breach in the process of open direct democracy.
- 3. Changes to constitutional rules must rigorously follow the democratic procedure.

## Article 4 — Reputation and contribution

- 1. Citizens can earn reputation through verified contributions, such as:
    - Identifying problems and proposing solutions.
    - Providing verifiable and reliable research and evidence.
    - Improving legislative proposals.
    - Identifying contradictions or unintended consequences.
    - Helping citizens understand complex subjects.
    - Contributing to the security, the integrity and the clarity of ODD.
- 2. Reputation can influence access to specialist responsibilities, moderation, research and advisory functions, but cannot increase the weight of an individual's fundamental vote. This is to prevents a system in which the most popular or experienced citizens gradually become a new political aristocracy.

## Article 5 — Transparency
- 1. Legislative proposals, discussions, voting results and decisions must be accessible to any citizen.
- 2. Public officials and administrative institutions must be accountable for their actions through transparent, simple, effective, and traceable procedures accessible to any citizen.
- 3. System rules and software should be open to independent inspection.
- 4. Citizens must be able to audit the integrity of decisions.
- 5. Privacy must nevertheless be protected. Public accountability does not mean exposing every citizen's identity or secret ballot.

## Article 6 — Protection against manipulation
- 1. ODD must protect itself against:
    - Fake accounts and automated voting.
    - Coordinated manipulation.
    - Bribery and vote purchasing.
    - Disinformation campaigns.
    - Foreign interference.
    - Cyberattacks.
    - Reputation manipulation.
    - Concentration of control over the platform.
    - Attempts to undermine democratic participation.
- 2. Security mechanisms must themselves remain transparent, independently auditable and subject to democratic oversight.

## Article 7 — Protection of minorities
**Democracy cannot simply mean that a majority may do anything it wants.**
- 1. Fundamental human rights cannot be abolished by an ordinary majority vote.
- 2. Citizens must have equal protection under the law.
- 3. Minority groups must retain their freedom of expression, association and participation.
- 4. Constitutional protections must apply equally to everyone.

## Article 8 — Administration without political rulers
**ODD distinguishes between making decisions and executing them.**

- 1. Citizens make the decisions. Public administration executes them.
- 2. Administrators should have clearly defined responsibilities, limited mandates, transparent performance records and mechanisms for removal or replacement.
- 3. Experts —regardless of who they are— who are recognized as such based on their reputations may offer advice and implement measures, but they must not acquire independent political authority.

## Article 9 — Constitutional evolution
**ODD must be capable of improving itself.**

- 1. Citizens may propose constitutional amendments, but these must be subject to:
    - a public consultation;
    - a public debate;
    - an independent impact assessment by experts recognized as such by the citizens;
    - a defined period of deliberation;
    - a direct vote by the citizens;
    - a public verification of the result.
- 2. Fundamental rights and democratic safeguards should be afforded stronger protection than that provided by ordinary law, which implies a need for greater citizen vigilance.

## Article 10 — The System Belongs to the People
- 1. The ODD platform must under no circumstances become the exclusive property or instrument of any individual or group of individuals, such as:
    - a political party;
    - a private company;
    - a government agency;
    - a technical development team;
    - a wealthy individual;
    - a group of privileged citizens.
- 2. Its infrastructure, governance rules, and decision-making mechanisms must remain accountable to the citizens.
